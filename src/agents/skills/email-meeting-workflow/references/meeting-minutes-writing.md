# Meeting Minutes and Follow-up Writing

Use this guide when the user asks for MOM, meeting minutes, a meeting recap, or a
post-meeting follow-up based on a transcript, uploaded audio, summary, or meeting record.

## Default behavior

- A bare MOM request produces minutes in the current conversation only.
- Do not assume the minutes should be emailed, saved to Drafts, or sent.
- After presenting the minutes, offer to prepare a customer-safe follow-up email.
- If the user asks to share the MOM, prepare the email and save it to the provider Drafts
  folder by default. Do not send without exact confirmation.
- If the request refers to an existing email chain and reply-draft tooling is unavailable,
  provide the reply copy but do not silently start a new thread.

## Required context

Resolve the meeting, customer or account, date, participants, purpose, transcript or audio,
meeting summary, and relevant deal context before writing. Prefer first-party meeting
evidence over generic CRM narrative. Never invent an owner, deadline, decision, commitment,
or customer statement.

## Default structure

Use the sections supported by the evidence:

1. Customer/account, meeting title, date, and purpose.
2. Participants.
3. Discussion summary grouped by topic.
4. Decisions and agreements.
5. Action items with owners and dates when explicitly stated.
6. Open questions, dependencies, or blockers.
7. Agreed next steps and next meeting, when known.

MOM and follow-up emails are allowed to be longer than the normal sales-email target. Favor
complete, scannable context over forcing the content into 60–120 words.

## Sharing boundary

Internal notes, risk labels, sales strategy, private transcript commentary, and unsupported
inferences may guide analysis but must not appear in customer-facing minutes. Rewrite the
content into neutral, evidence-backed language appropriate for all named recipients.
